# Outreach playbook — what we've learned about posting this work

**Started 2026-08-29**, from Post 01's measured performance and the drafting decisions that followed.
Update this after every post; the point is that the reasoning survives even when the drafts don't.

## 2. What those numbers actually mean

**98% out-of-network — assume the reader is a stranger.**
The algorithm pushed this far past the follower graph, so almost nobody arrives with context. **The
first line carries the entire post.** It has to be useful or arguable on its own, with no setup. Open on
a claim, not on a credentials statement or a funnel statistic. Credibility numbers still matter — they
just belong in the *second* paragraph, doing a different job.

**32 saves against 5 reposts — this audience treats the work as reference, not conversation.**
Saves are high-intent and low-broadcast: *"useful to me, I'll come back to this."* Six times the
reposts. Combined with only 31 comments against 118k impressions, the read is clear: people are
**filing** this, not debating it.

> **Therefore: favour concrete, re-readable specifics** — named mechanisms, real numbers, things
> someone could act on Monday — **over provocation.** A list of recommendations is close to the ideal
> shape for this audience. Chasing comments is the lower-yield play.

**63 followers and 318 profile views from one post — the credibility signal worked.**
People who engaged went looking for more. Whatever established authority in Post 01 should be kept.
In practice that's: specific numbers, named sources, and admitting limitations.

**0.4% click-through is not evidence that links fail.**
It is evidence the post **closed the curiosity gap it should have opened.** The fix is a better gap, not
fewer links. See §3.

## 3. Teaser vs standalone — the distinction that took two rewrites

The article/teaser model only works if the post leaves something unresolved.

> **Naming a finding creates a gap. Explaining it closes one.**

- **Too little** (a 219-word draft): named three findings without landing any. Buys a click, but leaves
  nothing behind when the click doesn't happen — and most won't.
- **Too much** (a 397-word draft): delivered the complete argument. A good standalone post and a poor
  teaser; there was no reason to click.
- **Right (~400 words):** each finding gets **a claim plus the evidence that makes it stick, then
  stops before it starts explaining.**

## 4. Calibration

| Format | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Post** | **~400 words, hard ceiling ~500** | long enough to make a point and tease |
| **Article** | 1,500–2,200 | may run long; it's the saveable artifact |
| **First line** | under ~140 chars | LinkedIn folds at ~210 desktop, ~140 mobile |

## 5. Moves that are working — keep them

**The self-implicating caveat.** *"I used a three-model panel to help tag this corpus. On the contested
calls it was right about half the time — in a review partly about the limits of machine agreement, that
applies to my own instrument too."* This is the single strongest credibility move available: it costs a
sentence, pre-empts the obvious objection, and demonstrates the discipline the work is arguing for.
**Always include the version of this that fits the post.**

**Closing with a real question.** Comments were the weakest metric. A genuine question costs one line.
Make it answerable from experience, not opinion — *"what actually reaches a human, and who decided
that?"* rather than *"what do you think?"*

**Graphics carry load.** Two exist in `assets/`: the screening funnel (credibility at a glance) and the
three-mechanism diagram (the argument). Both are 2400×1350 with editable SVG sources. Build images that
*make* a point rather than decorate one.

## 6. Framing rules for this body of work

- **The subject is oversight of AI *coding*, not AI code *review*.** Review is one mechanism inside it,
  alongside gates, routing, autonomy levels, provenance and evidencing. Getting this wrong narrows the
  whole thesis. *(Caught and fixed 2026-08-29 after it had propagated through three drafts.)*
- **Lead on scaling *human oversight*, not on tooling.** The question is how a human stays meaningfully
  in control of work they can no longer read all of — not which AI checking design wins. Every finding
  should answer "so what does a human still need to look at?"
- **Never publish an unsettled number.** The "1 of 72 papers clears the strict inversion test" figure is
  *not* publishable while 144 panel flags are unadjudicated. Use the qualitative phrasing until the
  closeout audit lands.
- **Fact-check every figure against the published version** of each paper before posting. Two papers in
  this corpus turned out to have preprint text attached to a published record (§148a).
